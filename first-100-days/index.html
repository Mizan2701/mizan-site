A note for new HR leaders

Five places a leader's picture of the workforce gets filtered

In your first 100 days, almost everything you learn about the workforce reaches you through someone else. A lot of it is accurate. Some of it has been softened along the way. These five places are where the softening usually happens.

  1. The engagement survey people don't fully trust

    If staff believe HR or their manager can see who said what, they answer safely. The scores look healthy, and they say very little.

  2. Results cut too thin

    A team of five isn't anonymous. People know that, so the smallest teams, often the ones under the most strain, go quiet.

  3. Exit reasons

    "Better opportunity" is the polite answer. It's rarely the whole one.

  4. Leadership's own expectations

    Ask your leaders to predict how their teams will score before you look at the results. The gap between the prediction and the result tells you more than either number alone.

  5. Records that never reach the sentiment report

    Overtime, leave taken, grievances raised and how quickly they closed. When records and survey answers disagree, the records are usually closer to the truth.

Five questions worth asking early

  • Who can see individual survey responses?
  • What is the smallest group we report on?
  • What do our last 20 exits have in common?
  • What do our leaders expect the results to show?
  • Do our records agree with what people tell us?
