Why risks stay hidden

Silence is not the same as satisfaction.

  • People fear being identified

    When a survey asks for an employee number or a login, honest answers stop. Staff answer the way that feels safe, not the way things are.

  • Concerns travel through the people they are about

    If the only route for a complaint runs through a direct manager, the most important complaints are the ones nobody makes.

  • Leaders see a different organisation

    Reports, dashboards and meetings show leadership a filtered picture. The bottlenecks that slow teams down are often visible only from the floor.

  • Nothing visibly changes afterwards

    A survey that leads nowhere teaches people that speaking up is pointless. The next one gets politer and less useful.

The approach

Three sources of evidence, compared side by side.

No single source tells the whole story. Mizan compares what employees experience, what the records show and what leaders believe, and pays most attention where they disagree.

Employee feedback

Everyone in scope, through an anonymous survey

Short factual statements (true or false) and experience statements (agree or disagree) across ten dimensions, plus a few questions for each workforce level. No written answers are collected.

Shows how work is actually experienced, team by team and level by level.

HR records and operational indicators

Documents your organisation provides, reviewed by Mizan

A structured check of twenty record types, such as contracts, payroll against attendance, leave balances, safety logs and complaint handling. Each is marked evidenced, partly evidenced or not evidenced.

Tests whether what people report is reflected in what the organisation can show.

Leadership predictions

Senior leaders, before any results are seen

Leaders estimate how employees will score each dimension and answer a few questions about how people risk is governed.

Reveals where leadership's picture differs from employees' experience.

Employees and records agreeThe finding is well supported. Act on it.
Employees and records disagreeSomething needs checking: a policy that exists on paper but not in practice, or a concern that needs more context.
Leaders and employees disagreeA perception gap. Often the most useful finding, and the starting point for an honest leadership conversation.

Method

How an assessment runs, and how it is scored.

  1. Agree the scope

    Workforce, sites, languages, records to review, privacy terms and timeline, all agreed in writing first.

  2. Leaders predict

    Senior leaders record their expectations before anyone answers.

  3. Employees answer, records are checked

    Anonymous survey on any phone or computer, alongside the records review.

  4. Report and plan

    Score, provisional rating, gap analysis and a prioritised plan presented to leadership.

Ten dimensions

  • Contract and role clarity
  • Pay integrity
  • Working time and rest
  • Leave and life events
  • Safety and physical wellbeing
  • Dignity and fair treatment
  • Voice, grievance and discipline
  • Security, mobility and exit
  • Leadership and management
  • Growth and recognition

The first eight are foundations tied to employers' legal obligations. The last two drive engagement and performance. Questions are mapped to the employment law of the country where your people work, and that mapping is reviewed by local counsel before any rating is issued.

How the score is built

  1. Each answer becomes 0 to 100: true or false statements score 100 or 0; the agree scale scores 100, 75, 50, 25 or 0. "Not sure" and "Don't know" are left out.
  2. A dimension score is the average of its statements, and the health score is the average of the ten dimensions.
  3. The rating comes from the band, then safeguards can only lower it.
A85 to 100Strong
B70 to 84Healthy
C55 to 69Needs attention
D40 to 54At risk
EBelow 40Critical

Safeguards: no rating below 60% participation; one or two red flags cap the rating at C, three or more at D; any foundation dimension below 40 caps it at D; a records evidence score below 50 marks the report unverified.

Limitations we state up front

  • Ratings and band thresholds are provisional. They will be recalibrated once results exist from at least ten organisations. There is no external benchmark yet.
  • A survey measures experience, not legal compliance. Mizan does not certify compliance or give legal advice.
  • A finding shows where to look, not who is at fault. Most findings need a follow-up conversation or record check before action is taken against any person or practice.
  • Results depend on participation. Low response rates, or groups too small to report, reduce what can be said.
  • Mizan is at prototype stage. Early assessments are run with participating organisations to test and improve the method.

Confidentiality and anonymity

Two different promises, kept separately.

Anonymous: employee survey answers

  • No name, employee number, email, phone or login is asked for.
  • Each person receives a one-time access code at random. The code is never stored with their answers.
  • Answers carry only role level and, optionally, department.
  • Results are reported only for groups of eight or more people.

Very small teams can still be recognisable from their level and department. That is why small groups are combined or hidden, and why department is optional.

Confidential: records, predictions and the report

  • HR records are reviewed by Mizan under a written agreement and are not published.
  • Leadership predictions are known to Mizan, so they are confidential rather than anonymous.
  • The report belongs to the organisation. Mizan does not share an organisation's name, score or findings with any third party.
  • No real employee responses or HR records are collected until consent wording, access and retention terms are agreed.

Questions leaders ask

Who takes part, and how long does it take?

Every employee in the agreed scope is invited. The survey takes most people around fifteen minutes on a phone or computer. Senior leaders also record their predictions before results are collected. Taking part is voluntary for employees, and they can skip any question.

How do you encourage honest participation?

Employees can see the protections before they answer, and leadership commits in advance to share what will change. A rating is only issued when at least 60% of people in scope respond.

Is it anonymous or confidential?

Both, for different parts. Employee survey answers are collected anonymously and reported only for groups of eight or more. HR records, leadership predictions and the final report are confidential: Mizan sees them, and the organisation decides who else does.

What does the report include?

A health score and provisional rating, results for each dimension and each workforce level, the perception gap between leaders and employees, the records evidence, any red flags, and a prioritised improvement plan with owners and review dates.

Do you share our results with anyone?

No. The report belongs to your organisation. Mizan does not share an organisation's name, score or findings with any authority or third party.

What does an early assessment cover?

Scope is agreed with each organisation: which workforce and sites, languages, the records to review and the timeline. Terms are discussed directly and confirmed in writing before any data is collected.

Can you work with organisations in any country?

Yes. The ten dimensions apply to any workplace. For each country, the questions and records check are mapped to its employment law, with local review before results are rated.
