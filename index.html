Why the truth stays hidden

Silence is not the same as satisfaction.

  • People fear being identified

    When a survey asks for an employee number, a login or even a department of five people, honest answers stop. Staff answer the way they think is safe, not the way things are.

  • Concerns travel through the people they are about

    If the only route for a complaint runs through a direct manager, the most important complaints are the ones nobody makes.

  • Nothing visibly happens afterwards

    One survey that leads nowhere teaches a workforce that speaking up is pointless. The next survey gets politer and less useful.

What it costs when nobody speaks

The problems you hear about last are the expensive ones.

Good people leave quietly

Resignations arrive with polite reasons. The real reason surfaced months earlier and never reached anyone who could fix it.

Disputes start outside the building

A grievance nobody heard inside becomes a formal complaint, a claim or a regulator's question, at far greater cost.

Compliance gaps go unnoticed

Unpaid overtime, missed rest days or unsafe practices are usually known on the floor long before they are known in the boardroom.

Leaders plan with the wrong picture

Decisions about pay, workload and promotion are made on what management believes, not on what employees experience.

Why Mizan works

Built so people can be honest, and leaders can act.

Most engagement surveys measure mood. Mizan measures whether the basics of a fair workplace are actually happening, in a way employees trust enough to answer truthfully.

Anonymity that holds up

No names, employee numbers, emails or logins. Each person receives a one-time code at random, and the code is never stored with their answers. Results are only shown for groups of eight or more.

Employees can see this for themselves before they answer, which is why they answer honestly.

Independent, not internal

Mizan sits outside your reporting lines. Your leadership receives findings, never individual responses, and nobody inside the organisation can trace an answer back to a person.

Leaders predict first

Before results are revealed, senior leaders record what they expect employees to say. The distance between belief and reality is often the most useful finding of all, and the hardest to argue with.

Grounded in labour law

Questions are built around the obligations employers already carry: contracts, pay, working hours, leave, safety, dignity, grievance and fair exit. Findings point to specific, fixable practices rather than vague sentiment.

The method

How an assessment runs.

A typical assessment takes four to six weeks from first conversation to final report. Employees spend about fifteen minutes on the survey.

  1. Agree the scope

    Workforce, departments, languages, privacy terms and timeline.

  2. Leaders predict

    Senior leaders record their expectations before anyone answers.

  3. Employees answer

    Anonymous one-time codes, on any phone or computer, in about fifteen minutes.

  4. Report and plan

    Score, rating, gap analysis and a prioritised plan presented to leadership.

Ten dimensions of workforce health

  • Contract and role clarity
  • Pay integrity
  • Working time and rest
  • Leave and life events
  • Safety and physical wellbeing
  • Dignity and fair treatment
  • Voice, grievance and discipline
  • Security, mobility and exit
  • Leadership and management
  • Growth and recognition

Each dimension combines factual statements (true or false) with experience statements (agree or disagree), plus a short set of questions specific to each workforce level.

Questions leaders ask us

Is it really anonymous?

Yes. Employees receive random one-time codes, and no name, employee number, email, phone or device detail is collected. Codes are stored only in scrambled form and are never saved alongside answers. Any group smaller than eight people is combined or hidden in the report.

Will employees actually take part?

Participation rises when people can see the protections for themselves and when leadership commits in advance to share what will change. We help you plan that announcement. A rating is only issued when at least 60% of staff respond.

Do you work outside the Gulf?

Yes. The ten dimensions apply to any workplace. For each country we map the questions to that country's labour law, so findings stay specific and actionable.

Do you share our results with anyone?

No. Your report belongs to your organisation. We never share an organisation's name, score or findings with any authority or third party.

What does it cost?

Fees depend on headcount and the number of sites. Founding partners receive reduced terms. We agree the fee on a short call once we understand your workforce.

How much of our people's time does it take?

About fifteen minutes per employee, once. Senior leaders spend a further ten minutes recording their predictions.
